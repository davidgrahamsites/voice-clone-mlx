# VoiceCloneGPT pilot recording script

This is a short recording-chain pilot, not the final ten-hour corpus. Use the
same microphone, room, distance, posture, and gain you intend to use later.
Record lossless mono WAV. Leave three seconds of silence after each spoken style
marker and two seconds between reading prompts.

Do not read the prompt IDs. Read the marker exactly once, wait, and then read
the prompts in that block.

## Calibration

Read this paragraph once at the beginning and once at the end:

The quick brown fox waited beside the quiet river while distant traffic faded
into the evening air. This calibration passage includes ordinary speech, soft
consonants, clear vowels, and a natural change in pitch.

---

## Neutral reading

Say: **This is the neutral reading.**

1. The studio opened early, so we had plenty of time to prepare.
2. I wrote down the address, checked the number, and saved the message.
3. Every useful idea begins as a question that someone is willing to explore.
4. The weather changed during the afternoon, but the road remained clear.
5. Please place the finished report in the shared folder before Friday.
6. A small improvement repeated every day can become a remarkable result.
7. The train leaves at seven forty-five and arrives just after nine.
8. Our audience includes beginners, experienced creators, and curious people.
9. The blue notebook contains the schedule, the budget, and the contact list.
10. I prefer careful work that remains useful long after it is finished.
11. The first chapter introduces the subject, while the second explains the method.
12. We can compare the options after we understand what each one requires.
13. The package weighs two point five kilograms and measures thirty centimeters.
14. Save a backup before changing the original file.
15. The meeting starts on Tuesday, March third, at ten thirty in the morning.
16. Some words sound different when they are spoken quickly in conversation.
17. A clear explanation should make the next action easier to understand.
18. The river was calm, although the wind moved through the trees.
19. I want the final version to be accurate, natural, and easy to listen to.
20. This recording is a test of the room, the microphone, and my speaking voice.

## Warm reading

Say: **This is the warm reading.**

1. I am glad you made it here, and there is no need to hurry.
2. Take a comfortable breath; we have plenty of time to work through this.
3. Thank you for sharing that idea with me.
4. Even a difficult day can contain one small moment of progress.
5. You do not have to solve everything at once.
6. I appreciate the care you put into making this useful for someone else.
7. Welcome back; it is good to have you with us again.
8. We can start with the simplest part and build from there.
9. Your questions are helpful because they show us where more clarity is needed.
10. Let us pause for a moment and notice how far we have already come.

## Energetic reading

Say: **This is the energetic reading.**

1. Today is a great day to begin something new!
2. We have a clear goal, a practical plan, and plenty of momentum.
3. Bring your best idea forward and let us see what it can do.
4. The next step is simple: choose a direction and start moving.
5. That result is faster, cleaner, and more useful than we expected.
6. There is real energy in a team that knows what it is building.
7. Open the project, press the button, and watch the process come alive!
8. We can make this clearer, stronger, and easier to use.
9. A bold experiment can reveal an opportunity that careful planning missed.
10. Let us turn this rough beginning into something people remember.

## Serious reading

Say: **This is the serious reading.**

1. Accuracy matters because other people may rely on this information.
2. We must protect the original material before making any changes.
3. Do not publish a result that has not been checked.
4. The decision affects the schedule, the budget, and the people involved.
5. If something is uncertain, say so clearly and record what remains unknown.
6. A reliable process is more valuable than a hurried success.
7. The evidence should be reviewed before we draw a conclusion.
8. We are responsible for understanding the consequences of this choice.

## Somber reading

Say: **This is the somber reading.**

1. The empty room felt different after everyone had gone home.
2. Sometimes the hardest part is accepting that a familiar chapter has ended.
3. The letter arrived too late to change what had already happened.
4. We remember the people who helped us, even when they are far away.
5. The rain continued through the night, quiet and steady against the window.
6. There are losses that cannot be repaired, only carried with care.
7. He looked at the old photograph and smiled despite the sadness.
8. Tomorrow may be uncertain, but it is still waiting for us.

## Questioning reading

Say: **This is the questioning reading.**

1. What would happen if we tried the smaller version first?
2. Did you save the latest copy before closing the application?
3. Which result seems most useful to you, and why?
4. Are we solving the right problem, or only the most obvious one?
5. How should the reader feel when this paragraph ends?
6. Could a shorter sentence make the instruction easier to follow?
7. When should we review the recording and compare the alternatives?
8. What do you want the next version to improve?

## Emphasis reading

Say: **This is the emphasis reading.**

1. This part is important: keep the original recording safe.
2. We need a result that sounds natural, not merely correct.
3. The most useful feature is the one that saves time every day.
4. Read the final sentence slowly and let the meaning settle.
5. Never confuse a confident voice with a certain answer.
6. The goal is simple: text in, your voice out.
7. Make the beginning clear, make the ending deliberate, and leave room to breathe.
8. One careful recording can improve every future generation.

## Dialogue reading

Say: **This is the dialogue reading.**

1. “Are you ready to begin?” she asked.
2. “Almost,” I said. “I want to check one more detail.”
3. “That makes sense,” he replied. “Let us take the time to do it well.”
4. “Could you send me the updated version?”
5. “Yes. I will send it before the end of the day.”
6. “What do you think?”
7. “I think we have found a better way to explain it.”
8. “Then let us write it down while the idea is still fresh.”

## Closing calibration

Read the calibration paragraph again, then stop recording and leave ten seconds
of room tone.
